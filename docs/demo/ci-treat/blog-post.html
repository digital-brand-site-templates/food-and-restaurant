The Three Ingredients We Never Run Out Of, And Why

Posted By: Admin

Ask any of our cooks what they'd save first in a fire and you'll get the same three answers: good olive oil, proper salt, and fresh herbs. It's not a glamorous list, and none of it photographs particularly well for a menu description — but almost everything that comes out of the kitchen leans on one of the three at some point, and running short on any of them changes how a dish tastes more than swapping out the headline ingredient ever would.

Salt isn't a seasoning you add at the end — it's a decision you make at every stage of cooking something.

Olive oil is the easiest to get wrong by overthinking it. We don't reach for the expensive bottle for everything — that one's for finishing a plate, not for the pan. What matters more is buying enough of a decent, everyday oil that nobody in the kitchen is ever tempted to stretch it thin. The same goes for salt: we use flaky sea salt at the table and coarser salt in the pot, and the difference in how a dish reads on the tongue is bigger than most people expect from something that costs so little.

Fresh herbs are the one place we've had to change habits over the years. Dried herbs have their place — a slow-cooked stew doesn't need much else — but anything that goes out fast, like a salad dressing or a finishing garnish, falls flat without something that was still growing that morning. That's part of why the specials board changes as often as it does: what's fresh dictates the plate more than any recipe card does.

None of this is a secret technique. It's closer to the opposite — it's the boring, repeatable groundwork that makes the more ambitious dishes on the menu actually land. Next time you're in, ask your server what came in freshest that morning. That's usually where the good dish is hiding.
